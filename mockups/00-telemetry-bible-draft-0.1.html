Telemetry

Base look under review

Clarity without ever sacrificing fun.

You've given every project this brief. This bible turns it into one set of rules for Home Assistant, Jam-Dash, Callsheet, JamFin, NervOS and Lobby. The default look, Telemetry, combines Dieter Rams's order, NASA's instrument habits, and the warmth of amber phosphor.

Sources: your answers in Rounds 1–2, plus interviews with all six project agents. Decision log: scratchpad/bible/decisions.md

Principles

01 · Readable first

The look never pays for itself with legibility

Readability loss is the most common complaint across all six projects. Personality lives in colour, motion, texture and motifs. Size, contrast and spacing stay fixed.

02 · Everything at a glance

Show it, don't hide it

No navigation that hides items, like Depth's arc ("atrocious"). Dashboards and launchers show every group on one screen. On the TV, rows of covers or screenshots can be carousels: they keep the screen calm, and the pictures do the talking.

03 · Less text

One title, one line

"Way too much text", "too long now haha", "TV is too much text". Each card says one thing. Help folds behind a "?".

04 · Amber looks, orange acts

Colour has jobs

Amber marks what you read, orange marks what you press. Green, amber and red are for state only. Idle controls stay neutral.

05 · Warm, never harsh

No pure white, no glare

"Too bright, cold and harsh" came up in four apps. Grounds are tinted, true black appears only behind video, and night screens stay mostly dark.

06 · Motion means something

Fast to move, cinematic to arrive

Moving focus is quick. Arriving somewhere gets the show. Each look gets one signature move. Nothing hitches. Loading is masked, not exposed.

State grammar

On is a solid lamp. Off is a hollow ring. Offline is a hatched lamp. Action pulses three times, then holds. Alert is a red diamond with a red rim. An unlocked door is now louder than a locked one, which closes an open HA audit item.

Motion grammar

The timings match what JamFin, Callsheet and NervOS already use, so adopting them is cheap. Cinema goes where you arrive somewhere, not where you browse.

Focus
150 ms · glide
Menu / popover
200 ms in · 150 out
Back
240 ms · leave
Screen
350 ms · settle
Scene (cinematic)
600 ms · settle

Scenes are opening a film, launching a game, booting, or changing sections. Each look gets one signature move for those moments, such as JamFin's Channel 3 launch or a mission-clock count-up. Menus pop above or below what they act on, like Unigrid's player menus ("use that everywhere").

Volume

There is one system. Each device turns the personality up or down: quiet on work tools, loud where you play.

Volume 1 · Desk

Quiet

Jam-Dash · Callsheet · HA desktop
  • Follows the device's light/dark setting
  • No grid, no grain; glow only in dark
  • Dense but calm; keyboard first
  • More motion than phone: hover, staggers, screen changes
Volume 2 · Touch

Present

HA phone & kiosks · JamFin mobile
  • Follows the device's setting; kiosks go dark at night
  • Room colours as stripes; big readouts
  • Restrained motion: short and touch-driven; one-shot staggers only on kiosks
Volume 3 · Lounge

Loud

Lobby · JamFin TV · NervOS
  • Always dark; less than 10% of the screen bright at night
  • Static grain and grid, focus glow, character art
  • One title and one line on screen; cinematic scenes
  • Carousels for cover and screenshot rows
