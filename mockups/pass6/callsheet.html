Callsheet, round 6: calm groups, a proper chat

Round 5's colours, type and feel, unchanged: the warm sheet by day, the navy by night, Jost and Plex Mono, walnut, one orange. What changed is how the information sits. The long continuous sheet is broken into quiet groups, one idea each, the way round 3's cards were. Ask is now a real conversation with its sources kept tidy beside each answer. Every other screen is brought to the same level. All names, mail, numbers and events are made-up sample data.

What changed since round 5

  • Groups instead of one sheet. Every screen is made of a few quiet panels on the sheet colour: a hairline edge, soft corners, a mono label, generous padding, one idea each. Today has five: First reply, Also waiting, Your day, Worth knowing, Coming up. Replies is grouped by why they wait.
  • Ask is a chat. Your questions sit in bubbles on the right, Callsheet's answers on the left, the composer is docked at the bottom, and earlier conversations are a list. Round 5's smart parts stay: numbered sources beside each answer, "How I found it", "Read 1 calendar and 1 thread", Remember this?, and a plain answer about what isn't true yet.
  • Almost no lights. The lit slot under the current place, the unlit windows on rows, the tick-box windows and the project windows are gone. A light now appears only for an alert, a clash and Ask while it reads. The current place is a short ink rule; on/off is a flat tick box.
  • Kept from round 5: one orange per screen that moves on (Copy → Done), the red alert key, "Said once, live wins", Undo in place, neutral keys for judgements, call times, the match gutter, the slip track, Setup with no orange at rest, and Read it to me.

1 · The flows, playable

3 · What's new or redrawn

The quiet groupA panel one step lighter than the ground by day and one step lighter than the navy by night, with a hairline edge, 16 px corners, a mono label at the top left and at most one quiet link at the top right. No shadow, no fill colour for state. A cue that needs you adds a 3 px edge in its state colour (red alert, amber clash), never a filled panel.
First replyToday's front door is one panel holding one person: who, why, what they want, the draft in an inset, and the one orange. An alert or a clash takes the same panel, and the reply waits on its Then line.
Grouped repliesReplies are grouped by why they wait (Due today, Asked you, Waiting a while), one panel each, so a list of seven reads as three short lists. The reply itself is two panels: what they want, then your draft.
The chatBubbles for you, panels for Callsheet. Each answer has its numbered sources beside it on desktop (hover a number to light its source) and folded under it on the phone. One quiet line under each answer: what it read, How I found it, Copy. The composer is docked; Ask turns orange only when there's a question in it.
Working, the one light in AskWhile Callsheet reads, its turn shows a lit slot and the source it's reading, with Stop. That's real status, so it keeps a light. Nothing loops: no dots, no spinner.
Flat tick boxes and option rowsSettings and choices use a flat tick box (ink fill when on) and option rows that press in. They're controls, not lights, so they don't use the indicator-window vocabulary.
