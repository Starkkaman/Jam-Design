Moving between the apps

"The purpose of this exercise is to create a design system where each interface design is bespoke to it's purpose, but moving between them feels natural." Isaac, 2026-11-05

Six evenings and mornings in the house, each crossing two or more apps. Every frame is the app's own pass-4 design at its real device size: Lobby, JamFin and NervOS on the living-room TV, Jam Home on the iPad and Echo kiosks and the phone, Callsheet and Jam-Dash on the phone and desk. Step through with → and ← (or the buttons). The striped steps are handoffs: both screens side by side, with what carries across, what changes, and where it jars. Nothing here is a new design: it's a test of the ones we have. All people, titles and data are samples.

The six threads a handoff should carry

From the 15-item language in notes/RULES-TIERS.md, these are the six you can actually see survive a move between two screens. Each handoff rates them: carries partly breaks not in play.

Seams to fix

Every jarring handoff noticed in the six journeys. Severity is about how much it costs someone moving between the screens, not how hard it is to build. "Tier" uses the tiers in notes/RULES-TIERS.md.

How this was made

Each frame copies its app's tokens and CSS fragments from mockups/pass4/ and is redrawn in fewer parts, so the screens are recognisable but not complete. TV frames are authored at 1920 × 1080 and scaled; the smallest TV text is 22 px at that size. Kiosk plans are placeholders (Jam Home's plan still needs Isaac's sketch). Where two pass-4 mockups disagree about a fact, the frame follows one and the seam table says so.
