Jam-Dash, pass 4: the equipment rack

Jam-Dash runs Docker apps on two servers, "home-server" and "nas". Its job is "is everything OK, and if not, fix it". Pass 4 drops the shared card template. Each server is drawn as a rack, each app is a module in it, the cords between them show which app needs which, and a strip of tape along the bottom keeps the last 24 hours. A board of fixed lights says, in one look, where the trouble is. All names, numbers and log lines are made-up sample data.

What changed since pass 3

  • No shared cards. Pass 3's ribbed faceplates and rounded tiles look like JamFin and HA. Here each server is a rack in a walnut-sided cabinet, and each app is a 1U or 2U module with ears and screw slots. 2U is for apps with a live preview.
  • The annunciator replaces the hero and the server strip. It's a fixed grid of lights: five subjects by two servers. When everything is fine, every light is dark. Its positions never change, so after a week you know where "nas · Disk" lives.
  • Trouble is a maintenance tag hung in the console, not a card. It keeps pass 3's grammar: light + label, title, one sentence, the fix underneath.
  • The patch field between the two racks shows the cords: which app needs which, and across servers. Cords show when you point at a module, and broken cords always show. "Why is Jellyfin down?" is answered by a dashed cord running to nas.
  • The event tape replaces the History link with the last 24 hours on two lanes, always in view. Pass 3's History page still exists behind it.
  • Kept from pass 3: the red alert key, one orange per screen in D23 order, warn = amber light + ▲, the first-screen promise, names that wrap instead of getting an ellipsis, step mode, and "live text steps".

1 · Home

Switch Situation above. Point at a module to see its cords. Click a lit light on the board to find its cause. Click an app's name for its detail (Esc or ‹ Home goes back). Press the red key to fix the alert: the next alert inherits the red key. Click a mark on the tape to read it. On the phone, the two server names at the top of the board choose which rack is shown.

Phone. The board comes first, so the phone gets the same glance as the desktop. Its two column heads pick the rack, and a lit square marks the one shown. The first tag shows in full, the rest fold into rows, and warns fold into "To look at". The rack below has 48 px modules, and every one is a target. The phone has no patch field (there's no room), so a waiting app names what it's waiting on. The tape sits at the end as one tap target that opens History.

Offline. A rack that can't be reached keeps its layout but turns to ghost modules: dashed outlines with the names as last seen and no states. The head unit says "Not answering since 01:12". On the other rack, everything patched to nas turns to warn, "Waiting on nas", and its cords run dashed amber across the field.

Press Update Jellyfin in either frame, and both frames follow. The steps are Check → Download → Swap → Watch → Done. The rack on the left shows the module working (a lit slot and the word), and the tape gets a mark.

4 · The new parts

Annunciator

Five subjects × two servers, in fixed places. Dark = fine, and the calm word stays readable in the recess. Lit red = down, amber + ▲ = needs a look, cream = something waiting for you (updates), dashed = not answering. Lit lights are links to their cause. They never toggle anything.

Rack and modules

A server is a rack and its head unit carries the server's own meters in a dark display window. Apps are 1U, or 2U with a live preview, in an order you choose. Ears with screw slots sit over the rails. The empty rack shows its rails, which is room to grow.

Pulled module

An app in alert slides 12 px out of line and casts a hard shadow, once (400 ms). It keeps its place in the rack, so nothing reorders. The reason stays on the tag (said once).

Patch field and cords

Each module has a jack on the ear facing the field. A cord is a cream line while you point at it. A broken cord is dashed amber and always visible. The cords come from mounts (nas shares), compose links and proxy hosts, and you can add one by hand in Edit setup.

Blanking plate

Stopped apps don't take rack space. They share one plate at the foot of the rack ("Stopped · 14"), which opens as a drawer of Start keys.

Maintenance tag

Trouble hangs in the console as a tag: a coloured stub, a punched hole, then light + label, title, one sentence and the fix. Fixed tags turn grey and stay till morning with "Fixed 01:53".

Event tape

A black ribbed band with the last 24 hours on two lanes. Red blocks are time down, amber is a warn, dashed is not answering, and cream ticks are changes (updates, restarts). The readout on the left says one event in plain words.

Bench

Updates and installs happen on a bench with a walnut lip, next to the rack they'll go into. The steps light slot windows, one orange per step, and the module in the rack shows the same job.
