Callsheet, pass 4: the day as a sheet you read

Isaac's new direction: one shared language, but each app's design is built for its purpose. Callsheet's purpose is a daily brief you read in a minute and act on in a few taps, so this pass drops the cards and the black band. The day is printed like a film call sheet crossed with a 70s flight plan: a time column, a running order read top to bottom, a numbered roster of who needs you, and a sign-off when the day is done. The functional rules from pass 3 stay. All names, mail and events are made-up sample data.

What changed since pass 3

  • No cards, no black band, no accent. The front door is one printed sheet with its own ground: a warm sheet by day, a deep navy sheet by night (the saved Mission palette). Hierarchy comes from type, rules and the time column, not from boxes.
  • The Next card became the roster's first row. Who needs you is a numbered roster, like a call sheet's cast list. Row 01 opens out with its draft and keys. When an alert or a clash takes the cue, row 01 shrinks and is labelled Then, so the Then line from pass 3 is the roster itself, not an extra line.
  • The day is a running order. Desktop prints the day down a time column with a NOW rule across it. Free time is printed too, as gaps, and the first gap says how many replies fit in it. On the phone the day folds into a one-line day strip; a tap unfolds it in Agenda, so the day is never drawn twice on one screen.
  • The sheet is the full brief. Pass 3's separate Full brief page is gone. Worth knowing and Coming up are printed under the roster, and the summary's sources are footnotes.
  • Kept from pass 3: the cue order (alert, offline, clash within 60 min, reply, calm), the red alert key, one orange per screen, "Just this one" for Not for me, Undo on everything, Read it to me, the single-letter keys, five places.

1 · The flows, playable

Pick a flow and step through it. Each step sets both frames, and both stay live: click rows and keys. Desktop keys (click the desktop frame first): C copy, D done, L later, O open, X not for me, W work through them, R read it to me, / find or ask, 1–5 places, J/K move, ? keys, Esc back.

3 · New elements

Running orderThe day printed down a time column with a hairline rail and a tick per event, like a flight plan's timeline. A NOW rule (a lit cream slot and a full-width line) cuts across the sheet. Finished events fold into one line.
Gap cuesFree time is printed as a dashed stretch of the rail ("Free until 11:00 · 55 min"). The first gap says how many replies fit in it ("Room for the first 3 on the roster, about 6 min"), so replies are scheduled into the day instead of piling up.
The rosterWho needs you, as a call sheet's numbered cast list: an ID box, the name, one line for what they want, the reason in words (no colour dots), the age. Row 01 opens out with the draft. The numbering is the count, so nothing else counts them.
Then rowWhen an alert or clash takes the cue, row 01 is labelled THEN and keeps a quiet Copy draft. The pass 3 Then line now costs no extra line.
Clash bracketTwo overlapping events share one time and an amber bracket in the rail, with the round amber light and "▲ Clash · pick one".
Day stripOn the phone, the running order folds into one line: a strip-board bar of the day with a needle for now. A tap unfolds it in Agenda.
Source footnotesEach sentence of the summary carries a small number. The numbers are explained at the foot of the sheet (which calendar or inbox it came from).
WrapWhen the roster is clear, the sheet signs off: "That's the day", three figures (answered, tomorrow, sent: always 0), a walnut double rule that draws in once, and tomorrow's first thing. No orange.
