NervOS in Braun Hi-Fi: the games shelf on the living-room TV

NervOS is the 10-ft games launcher on the Aero 15 under the 65" TV. It plays retro games in emulators, starts Steam games, streams from other computers and plays films. This pass draws it as the house look: Home with Continue, Collections and a systems carousel, a shelf, the game page, the launch title card, and the quick menu over a paused game. Click the TV and use the arrow keys, Enter (A / OK), Esc (B / Back), G (Guide) and [ ] (L1 / R1), or the remote buttons. All games, art and computers are made up.

What's on it, and where it came from

Home is "what we play" (Steam Deck)Steam's Gaming Mode opens on a recent-games shelf, with the library one step away. Here Home opens on Continue (all sources mixed), then Collections, then every system. The four NervOS sections stay as tabs in the band, in plain words: Retro, Steam, Streaming, Films.
Collections first, systems second (ES-DE)ES-DE's automatic collections (All games, Favourites, Last played) and custom ones are how people browse once a library grows. Systems become one carousel row with the neighbours showing and "3 of 8". Collections are drawn as a walnut shelf of spines, not a grid of logos.
Quick menu in reach (Batocera, Steam Deck)Batocera puts save, load, slot and screenshot on hotkey chords; Steam Deck puts its overlay on one button. Guide opens one band with the same order: Resume, Save, Load, Slot, Picture, Reset, Quit. Reset and Quit need a hold, with the hold filling on the key's top edge.
Not a cockpitNo codes (0003/0011), no kanji, no hex grid, no hazard stripes. Systems show their media (a cartridge, a disc, a board) because Isaac asked for launch screens that "show their media". Text is one title and one line.
One selectorCards: the base window on the plinth's bottom edge. Lists and keys: the same window on the leading edge. Films keep their progress on the art's bottom edge, so progress and the selector never share an edge.
Title card holds only as long as the loadShutter + slatted bar, flat. The bar shows the emulator (or Steam, or the stream) starting, with a 600 ms floor so the shutter can finish. Calm makes it a 140 ms fade.
