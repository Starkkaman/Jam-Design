NervOS in Braun Hi-Fi, pass 2: Archive, Hangar, Uplink, Theater

The 10-ft games launcher on the Aero 15 under the 65" TV: retro games in emulators (Archive), Steam games (Hangar), streams and netplay (Uplink) and films (Theater). Pass 2 keeps NervOS's approved section names, fits more of Home on screen, marks where the systems carousel wraps, shows each value once in the Guide band, gives Reset and Quit a press-twice path, opens the same Guide band in Steam games and streams, and tests the layout against busy, varied covers. Click the TV, then use the arrow keys, Enter (A), Esc (B), G (Guide) and [ ] (L1 / R1), or the pad buttons. All games, covers, computers and films are made up.

Section names on the band: NervOS's approved names, in Jost sentence case with no numbers or kanji. The page switch above shows plain words as an alternative only.

What changed since pass 1

Approved names keptArchive, Hangar, Uplink and Theater stay on the band. They're set like VHS's tabs: friendly Jost, sentence case, no 01–04 codes, no kanji. Each section's sign says in one plain line what's inside. Plain words are a labelled page switch, not the design.
More of Home on screenSmaller cards (288 px, almost six across, labels still 26 / 22 px) and a sign that's just title + one line: about 2.6 rows are visible instead of 1.8, and the next row always peeks.
The carousel shows its wrapA walnut bookend marked "Start" sits where the last system meets the first. A held → stops at the bookend; a fresh press goes round.
Each value once in the Guide bandSave and Load share one tray that names the slot once. While a chooser (slots, picture) is open, its key hides its value and the chooser's lit square carries it.
Hold, or press twiceReset, Quit and replacing a save state: hold A for a second, or press A, read "Press A again", and press again. The HA remote can't hold, so it uses the second path.
One Guide band everywhereIn a Steam game, a stream or a film, Guide opens the same band with fewer keys: Resume, the content's own menu, Quit. The covers are now eight kinds of made-up art, from bright paper to saturated red, to test busy real covers.
