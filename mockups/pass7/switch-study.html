Jam design · Round 7 · Switch study

The channel-reveal switch

Isaac, after round 6: the others could be smaller switches that have an accent color in the channel that is reveled when it's on. Could be an interesting subtle accent in other designs as well. This page draws that switch once, as a shared part: what it's made of, its states, sizes and motion, how it sits in Callsheet, JamFin, Jam-Dash and Lobby in each app's own colours, and where it shouldn't be used. Every switch on the page works. Nothing here changes any app.

1 · Anatomy

A channel, a reveal and a thumb

Drawn at four times phone size, Day on the left and Night on the right. Tap either to watch the reveal.

2 · States

Off, on, pressed, disabled, unavailable, and two kinds of focus

Phone size (46 × 28). The TV focus is shown on a Lobby row at the same scale. "Pressed" is the thumb shrinking in place while a finger is down.

3 · Motion

The reveal is the signature: the colour follows the thumb

The thumb moves with transform; the accent opens with clip-path from the left edge, so it seems to come out from behind the thumb. 180 ms on the house settle curve cubic-bezier(.16,1,.3,1). Nothing fades, glows or bounces. With Calm (or the system's reduced motion) the switch changes at once.

Frames at 0, 25, 50, 75 and 100 % of the way, Day.

Full · 180 ms

Kiosk size, Night. Set Motion to Calm at the top and run it again.

4 · Sizes

Phone, kiosk, desktop, TV

Drawn at 1:1 in CSS px (TV in 1080p px). The dashed box is the hit area: the switch is small, the target isn't. Proportions stay the same: width = 1.65 × height, thumb = height − 2 × gap.

5 · In the other apps

The same switch as a quiet accent, in each app's own colours

Same channel, same thumb, same motion. Only the revealed colour changes, and each comes from that app's palette, fitted to the same lightness so contrast holds. The switch replaces only things that are already plainly on/off (a checkbox that applies at once, or an On/Off choice). Choices with three or more options stay as keys.
